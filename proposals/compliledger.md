## Abstract

Institutional assurance remains heavily manual, fragmented, periodic, and document-driven, with point-in-time assessments repeatedly assembled for audits, regulators, counterparties, and operational workflows. Meanwhile, financial infrastructure is becoming digital, tokenized, programmable, interconnected, and increasingly automated. Assurance must therefore do more than report a past condition: it must respond when the requirements, evidence, or operational conditions supporting a decision change.

CompliLedger is AI-Powered Proof Infrastructure. It brings requirements, target, context, and operational state into a continuous process that determines applicable controls, orchestrates and validates evidence, evaluates evidence sufficiency, and produces deterministic assessments and decisions. AI assists interpretation and evidence orchestration; machine-readable controls and deterministic evaluation logic produce consequential decisions, without forcing a satisfaction judgment when evidence is insufficient. Portable Decision Packages and Canonical Proof Packages make these outputs consumable and machine-verifiable, supporting independent verification and continuously updated assurance.

The operating-model shift is from manual to AI-native, periodic to continuous, and point-in-time reporting to real-time or near-real-time assurance within defined operating conditions. Material changes trigger evidence refresh and reassessment; updated decisions and proof lifecycle states distinguish current assurance from stale or superseded results. Remediation can then produce newly supported assurance. This moves audit preparation toward ongoing audit readiness and report-based assurance toward machine-verifiable proof, replacing one-time assessments with reusable, continuous operational assurance rather than merely adding a ledger commitment to a static report.

Canton is particularly well suited to this application-layer infrastructure because institutional multi-party financial workflows require privacy, authorization, deterministic coordination, selective information sharing, and composability across applications. The project will make assurance and its verification consumable alongside financial workflows while keeping sensitive evidence in appropriate protected systems rather than broadly publishing it to a ledger. CompliLedger complements Canton and authoritative enterprise systems; it does not replace the protocol, systems of record, auditors, or regulators.

CompliLedger already exists as a functioning platform, with a limited-control proof of concept and a built ProofSync portal; the complete Canton-native implementation is not yet delivered. The Development Fund project funds the complete reusable Canton-native integration and ecosystem infrastructure—not the invention of CompliLedger or the entire private commercial platform. It combines Canton-native assurance and proof architecture, deterministic decision integration, continuous reassessment, portable decision/proof packages, independent verification, and governed workflow consumption with one primary SDK, documented interfaces, and Canton-specific integration for ProofSync, AuditSync, RegSync, and DevSync. Two reusable references—Institutional Continuous Assurance and Tokenized RWA / Settlement—will demonstrate changing assurance and infrastructure reuse. A bounded agentic financial-governance extension will exercise authority, delegation, human approval, and execution lineage without becoming a separate product or the primary project justification.

Canton participants will receive reusable integration components, specifications, schemas, developer tooling, verification tooling, and reference workflows that make continuous assurance consumable by internal teams, clients, counterparties, auditors, regulators, developers, financial applications, and appropriately governed automated workflows. Shared interfaces to CompliLedger's applicability, evidence orchestration, control evaluation, and decision capabilities, together with reusable assurance lifecycle and verification infrastructure, are intended to reduce duplicated implementation and repeated audit preparation. Authorized third parties should be able to integrate the shared components, consume supported packages, independently verify supported properties, and follow lifecycle updates without a proprietary portal or bespoke employee assistance. Reuse remains conditional on applicability, context, authorization, evidence freshness, and stakeholder requirements; a proof does not automatically satisfy every regulation or consumer.

The approved working funding request is $385,000 USD, based on bottom-up delivery costing, for an approximately 15-month program: approximately nine months through accepted production launch, followed by six complete months of bounded maintenance and ecosystem adoption support. Funding includes external security review, remediation and retesting, production-readiness validation, staged deployment, documentation, and external developer validation and onboarding—not only initial integration. Production deployment remains subject to verified Canton requirements and the approved topology; the maintenance period begins after accepted production launch and is not consumed by implementation delays.
